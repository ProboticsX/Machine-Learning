- Dow Jones Today: Stocks Plunge, Treasury Yields Surge as Investors Monitor Tax Bill

The stock market witnessed a significant drop on May 21, 2025, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average plunging by nearly 800 points, a 1.9% decline. The market turmoil was driven primarily by concerns over a comprehensive tax and spending bill progressing through Congress, which could substantially increase the federal deficit. Treasury yields, particularly the 10-year Treasury note, surged to levels not seen since February, reaching 4.60%, translating to increased borrowing costs. Key contributors to the market's decline include UnitedHealth, which fell almost 6% amid reports of secret payments to nursing homes to reduce hospital transfers. Other notable declines were seen in Target, Lowe's, and Palo Alto Networks despite some companies posting better than expected earnings. In contrast, Alphabet gained nearly 3%, buoyed by optimism around its AI advancements, alongside Bitcoin hitting a record high before fluctuating.

- Trump's crypto dinner cost over $1 million per seat, on average

More than 200 wealthy crypto buyers paid hefty sums to attend a black-tie dinner with President Donald Trump, with total expenditures on Trump's official cryptocurrency $TRUMP reaching $394 million. Winners of a contest to meet Trump each spent between $55,000 and $37.7 million, with an average spending of almost $1.8 million. Approximately 72% of attendees appear to be foreign nationals, raising concerns regarding U.S. laws prohibiting foreign campaign donations. Trump-affiliated companies own 80% of the $TRUMP token, which profits through transaction fees. Despite ethical questions raised, the White House defends the president’s actions as serving American interests. The event illustrates how Trump's business interests are intertwined with his presidency, leveraging exclusive events for personal gain.

- Cigna announces new deal for copay caps on Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk weight loss drugs

Cigna's Evernorth unit announced a new agreement with pharmaceutical giants Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk to cap monthly out-of-pocket costs at $200 for popular GLP-1 weight loss drugs Wegovy and Zepbound. This move aims to widen employer coverage and reduce costs for insured workers. Currently, only half of Cigna’s clients offer coverage due to high drug prices. The deal also simplifies pre-authorization processes and enables access to these drugs at consistent prices across retail pharmacies and home delivery services. The pricing deal may reduce costs by nearly 20% for current plan sponsors, complementing recent initiatives like CVS Caremark designating Wegovy as its preferred drug and upcoming Medicare negotiations expected to further lower prices.

- Nike will raise prices on a wide range of products as soon as this week

In response to anticipated tariff impacts on profits, Nike is set to increase prices on numerous adult footwear, apparel, and equipment products, effective as early as the week of May 21, 2025. Price hikes range from $2 to $10, with footwear priced above $150 seeing the highest increases. Notably, prices on children's products and popular items like Nike's Air Force 1 will remain unchanged as the company aims to minimize financial burden on families. Nike is adjusting prices as part of regular business evaluations amid challenges posed by new tariffs on goods from China and Vietnam. The increases will reflect in Nike’s stores and website, with broader retail impacts yet to be seen.

Relevant links: [https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/21/nike-price-increases-tariffs.html]

- Why are more shoppers struggling to repay 'buy now, pay later' loans?

Increasing numbers of shoppers are facing difficulties repaying 'buy now, pay later' (BNPL) loans, a trend highlighted by Klarna’s recent disclosure of a 17% rise in consumer credit losses in Q1. BNPL plans, which enable installment payments often without interest or credit checks, are popular among younger consumers and minority groups but are raising concerns over potential overextension of credit. Studies suggest BNPL usage can lead to increased spending, overdraft fees, and credit card debt. Regulatory scrutiny has decreased under the current federal administration, raising warnings from consumer advocates about reduced protections. The challenges reflect broader financial stress, exacerbated by rising costs of living and a record high national consumer debt.
