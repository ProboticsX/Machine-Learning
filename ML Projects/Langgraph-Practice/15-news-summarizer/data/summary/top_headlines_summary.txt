- "Final Destination Bloodlines," the sixth installment in the horror franchise, achieved a franchise-best opening day with $21 million across 3,523 locations, surpassing the previous series high set by the fourth film. This success is buoyed by the movie's availability in premium formats such as IMAX and large-format auditoriums, combined with solid critical reviews and a favorable audience score of B+. The relatively modest $50 million production budget places "Bloodlines" in an advantageous position for profitability, helped further by an international gross of $26.4 million so far. On the other hand, The Weeknd's psychological thriller "Hurry Up Tomorrow" experienced a disappointing debut, earning just $2.33 million on opening day in 2,020 theaters. The film, linked to The Weeknd’s ongoing global touring and produced by a coalition of music and entertainment companies, garnered poor reviews and a "C-" audience grade, limiting its theatrical prospects. Other films such as "Sinners," which continues strong into its fifth weekend, and Marvel's "Thunderbolts*" maintain steady earnings, while family-friendly and sequel titles hold their places in the box office rankings.

- Angelina Jolie, serving as the Chopard Godmother at the 2025 Cannes Film Festival, presided over the glamorous Chopard Trophy dinner held at Carlton Beach. The event honored rising stars Marie Colomb and Finn Bennett, recognized for their versatility and empathy in global cinema. Jolie used her platform to underscore the power of international cinema to foster empathy by connecting audiences to diverse human experiences worldwide, including the harrowing realities faced by artists in conflict zones. She paid tribute to artists who lost their lives sharing their stories, accentuating the humanitarian dimension of the festival beyond its traditional glamour. The gala was attended by an array of high-profile film industry personalities including Quentin Tarantino, Halle Berry, and Juliette Binoche, highlighting the occasion’s blend of celebrity presence and advocacy for global cinema accessibility.

- At the criminal trial of Sean Combs, Dawn Richard, former member of Danity Kane, testified regarding a violent 2009 incident where she witnessed Combs assaulting his ex-girlfriend Casandra "Cassie" Ventura. Richard detailed aggressive behavior including an attack involving a skillet filled with hot eggs, placing Ventura in danger and causing her to fall to the ground in fear. This testimony supports earlier accounts by Ventura and forms part of the prosecution’s broader argument about a criminal enterprise engaged in abuse and intimidation. Richard also alleges her own experiences of physical and psychological abuse by Combs, underlining a pattern of coercion and control. Combs denies all accusations and asserts his innocence. The trial continues amid significant public attention due to the serious nature of the allegations covering sex trafficking, racketeering, and abuse.

- "Urchin," the feature directorial debut by British actor Harris Dickinson, premiered at the Cannes Film Festival to an enthusiastic five-minute standing ovation. The film explores the plight of homelessness and mental health struggles through the life of Mike, portrayed by Frank Dillane, a rough sleeper in London trying to break free from a cycle of self-destruction after incarceration. Produced by BBC Film alongside BFI and others, "Urchin" showcases a raw, sometimes absurd depiction of societal challenges, marking Dickinson's successful transition from actor to filmmaker. His presence and the film's reception at Cannes underscore his growing influence in international cinema, supported by notable appearances from fellow actors including Paul Mescal.

- The 2025 Cannes Film Festival has introduced a new dress code that bans nudity and excessively long dress trains on the red carpet, signaling a notable shift in the prestigious event's fashion standards. This marks a departure from the festival’s history of bold and daring fashion moments, exemplified by iconic gowns worn by stars such as Bella Hadid, Kendall Jenner, and Deepika Padukone in previous years. The changes are motivated by considerations of decency and practical concerns over voluminous trains impeding movement. The revised guidelines indicate a movement towards more modest, wearable fashion at Cannes, balancing the event's glamorous reputation with a desire for decorum and accessibility. The new dress code effectively prohibits some of the most provocative and extravagant styles that have historically defined the festival's red carpet.
