- Doom: The Dark Ages, the latest installment in the iconic Doom video game series, faces the challenge of attracting new players in 2025's competitive gaming landscape. Despite its long-standing reputation and loyal fanbase since its debut in 1993, Doom competes against free-to-play giants like Fortnite and Roblox, which dominate player attention. The new medieval sci-fi setting and gameplay changes aim to appeal to a broader audience, especially newcomers. Unlike subscription or microtransaction-based games, Doom offers a one-time purchase without additional costs, which developers argue provides clear value. While pricing for premium games has remained relatively stable when adjusting for inflation, ongoing debates about game costs continue, especially against the backdrop of tariff concerns and the rise of subscription services. The game is also available on subscription platforms like Xbox Game Pass, challenging traditional sales models but offering accessibility. With features such as customizable difficulty and replay value, Doom: The Dark Ages seeks to stay relevant in an evolving market by blending familiar elements with fresh innovations.

- Privacy and online anonymity remain challenging issues as illustrated by a test of the "Cover Your Tracks" tool by the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF). This tool measures how well a user’s browser blocks tracking attempts and how unique their browser fingerprint is. While modern browsers like Safari and Firefox include features to block ads and trackers effectively, they struggle to prevent browser fingerprinting—a practice where trackers use device and browser information to uniquely identify users across the web. The test revealed that even with privacy settings enabled and ad blockers installed, browsers can be easily singled out, limiting true anonymity. Techniques to avoid fingerprinting, such as using the Tor browser or privacy extensions like Privacy Badger, can improve privacy but may reduce web functionality or user experience. This nuanced balance underscores the ongoing difficulty in fully protecting online identity against sophisticated tracking technologies.
